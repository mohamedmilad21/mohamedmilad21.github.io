Work & history

What I've actually shipped.

Product Manager at Presto since June 2025 — a delivery platform operating across Libya and Tunisia. This is the honest version of the CV: the projects, the decisions, and the parts that didn't go to plan.

Customer app

PrestoMan (driver)

Partner app & Business Manager

Timeline

June — August 2025

Launching Presto in Tunisia

My first real mission: help take a product built for Libya into a second country, against an incumbent that already owned the market.

  • Ran a detailed competitive teardown of the incumbent's customer and driver apps, and turned it into a differentiator argument rather than a feature copy-list.
  • Built the Tunisia metrics dashboard and documented every existing dashboard feature and process, so the new market had a baseline from day one.
  • Drove localisation: French and Arabic UX language revisions, IP-based country code and map pin defaults, currency handling, and localised banners.
  • Pushed for guest-by-default onboarding with cart restore on sign-up, so acquisition wasn't gated behind a login screen.
  • Talked to customers, drivers and partners on the ground, and documented local behaviour and category preferences instead of assuming Libyan habits transferred.

June — September 2025

The partner side: Business Manager, coupons and reporting

Owned the specs for the tools partners and business managers use every day.

  • Wrote the partner reporting spec around metrics admins and business managers actually needed — after finding out the real job to be done was generating monthly financial reports, not browsing charts. One consolidated table replaced a pile of separate exports.
  • Specified the coupons system end to end after researching how mature commerce platforms model it: coupon types (basket, delivery fee, product), budget caps instead of usage counts, a maximum discount value on percentage coupons to stop a campaign burning its budget in a day, and free-delivery coupons for new customers.
  • Deliberately kept the partner statistics page to one simple page — partners had asked for clarity, not a control panel.
  • Compensation and dispute amounts in the dashboard: defined the difference between money refunded to a customer at the partner's cost and money returned to a partner after a wrong compensation.

August — October 2025

Hypermarket integrations and delivery performance

  • Ran the Geant hypermarket integration into Benghazi: catalogue matching by barcode, image URL ingestion, category cleanup, and live production test orders on the ground.
  • Found and escalated the real constraint — an API-only integration meant the partner couldn't accept or reject orders — instead of shipping around it quietly.
  • Analysed the new order-dispatching logic: batched orders were still taking nearly twice as long as single orders, improving only from 13.47 to 13.13 minutes. The honest read was that batching wasn't paying for itself yet.
  • Separated restaurant wait time from total customer delivery time, so we stopped arguing about one number that hid two different problems.
  • Proposed moving from manual, ops-driven dispatch settings to a rules-based automatic assignment system, and wrote the criteria questions — distance between pickups, orders per journey, order value ceilings, time windows — that the logic would need.

August 2025

Hacking Growth in fifteen days

Set as a development goal ahead of my evaluation: read a 271-page growth book in fifteen days and present it back to the team. It's where activation, retention and the north-star framing stopped being vocabulary and started being how I read our own numbers.

November 2025

Rolling out how the product team works

  • Communicated the six-week cycle and two-week cooldown to the wider business — including when to submit requests so they'd actually be shaped in time for the next cycle, and that some things would be frozen mid-cycle for technical reasons.
  • Wrote PRDs for the customer order invoice and for letting admins revert a cancelled order to delivered.
  • Learned to build the guard dashboard first: before running a sign-up experiment, put a live sign-up tracker in place so a drop would be visible immediately rather than at the end of the test.

December 2025

My first betting table

  • Given a week to shape three PRDs to betting-table standard: problem space, customer and business pain with evidence, proposed solution, expected impact and success metrics, plus competitor research.
  • Ran discovery across stakeholder requests, support tickets and data to pick which three problems were worth the room's time — the hardest part of the whole exercise.
  • Presented into a betting table with the strategic manager, CTO, engineering director and product lead. We came out with the ledger system and a redefinition of direct payments among the bets.
  • Ran the documentation and the meeting for a partner integration discovery session the same month.

January — March 2026

Integrations, trust and the block-dispute flow

  • Owned partner onboarding onto the vendor integration API — test environments, credentials, webhook verification, and end-to-end order tests with partner engineering teams in another country.
  • Shipped the block-dispute flow: customers who'd been blocked could finally see it and contest it, surfaced at the moment they hit the wall rather than buried in a feedback form.
  • Worked on phone number masking and order-ID protection for customer and driver privacy.
  • Got pushed hard, and rightly, on prioritisation: why is this a priority at all, regardless of the solution? and how many orders would actually be affected? That question now goes at the top of every brief I write.

April — September 2026

Leading, shaping, and handing work on

  • Lead a product team of PMs, designers and data engineers, and run the internship and trainer programme — three colleagues I trained are now onboarded on the team.
  • Own shaping during each cooldown: at least two projects brought to a shapeable state per cycle.
  • Business Manager foundations, automated financial breakdowns to partners, and market-specific cancellation reasons for Tunisia.
  • Explored combining personas, offers and notifications into a single campaign capability for the operations team.
  • Learned the unglamorous half of the job: chasing finance, engineering and operations across three threads at once so a feature actually reaches production.

How I'd describe myself to a hiring manager

I'm a Product Manager who works close to the operation. I've launched a product into a second country, specified the tools that thousands of partner and driver interactions run through, and learned to defend a prioritisation call in a room with the CTO in it.

I'm strongest at discovery and shaping — turning a vague stakeholder request into a problem with evidence attached. I'm still growing at experimentation design and at long-range strategy, which is exactly why they're marked Level 2 on my skill tree rather than rounded up.
